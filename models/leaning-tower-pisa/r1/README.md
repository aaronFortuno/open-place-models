# Torre pendente di Pisa · r1

Pisa, Italy. The model covers the freestanding campanile of the cathedral, with a circular plinth, six rows of loggias and a smaller open bell chamber. Its 15.5 m ground diameter and the highest point at 56.70 m follow the above-ground dimensions reported in the 2014 radar study. The Opera della Primaziale Pisana gives 58.36 m without specifying the datum. The ICOMOS survey reports a comparable 58.4 m from the foundation ring to the centre of the upper terrace. The mesh uses a flat ground contact and omits the buried foundation and surrounding sunken walkway. [Custodian specifications](https://www.opapisa.it/visita/torre-pendente/) · [ICOMOS height datum](https://www.icomos.org/public/publications/laussane-47.pdf) · [2014 radar study](https://academic.oup.com/jge/article/11/5/055004/5110272)

## Lean and exterior geometry

- **Direction and date.** The model uses a true bearing of 180° as an approximation to the near north–south survey section, with the tower leaning south. The 2021 Regione Toscana 1 m surface model gives about 4 m of southward displacement for upper pixels and a smaller westward component. This independently confirms the direction at map scale. Its resolution limits the precision of the bearing. [2009 stabilisation study](https://geocasehistoriesjournal.org/issues/volume-1-3/leaning-tower-of-pisa-behaviour-after-stabilization-operations) · [Regione Toscana 2021 surface tile](https://www502.regione.toscana.it/geoscopio/download/altimetria/DSM_autocorrelazione_2021/DSM_273052.zip)
- **Offset and angle.** Figure 5 of Amato, Antonucci and Belnato's May 2001 survey gives horizontal centre offsets of 2.27, 3.25 and 3.93 m at heights of 26, 40 and 47 m. The model applies an approximate 200 arcsecond northward rotation to those points, based on about 1,800 arcseconds of cumulative correction by June 2001 and about 2,000 arcseconds by 2022. This gives about 2.24, 3.21 and 3.88 m south. The estimated crown centre is 4.30 m south at 56.28 m, giving a base-to-crown chord of 4.37° from vertical. The 2014 study reports an inclination of 3.97° for the tower, and the 2009 paper describes a foundation angle of 5.5° before stabilisation. The model uses a curved axis, so local floor slopes and the base-to-crown angle describe different measurements. [2001 survey, figures 3, 5 and 6](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/xxxiv/5-W12/proceedings/04.pdf) · [2009 stabilisation study](https://geocasehistoriesjournal.org/issues/volume-1-3/leaning-tower-of-pisa-behaviour-after-stabilization-operations/pdf) · [2022 monitoring update, figure 11](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781003308867-49) · [2014 radar study](https://academic.oup.com/jge/article/11/5/055004/5110272)
- **Arcades and chamber.** The six loggia intervals, differing floor slopes and set-back belfry follow the published section. The model retains the 15 blind arches at the base and 30 columns per loggia as relief. The relief fills the thin gap behind the columns so that they are supported at map scale. The belfry preserves six through-openings and an open top. Small upper arches, individual capitals, bells, railing and interior stair geometry are omitted. Belfry openings, wall thickness and relief details are approximated for printing. [2001 survey, figure 6](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/xxxiv/5-W12/proceedings/04.pdf) · [Structural survey of the loggias](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-874X(01)00074-9) · [Pisan Romanesque belfry description](https://www.pisanromanesquemeets.it/teaching-materials/the-leaning-tower/)

## Registration, source and reuse

The anchor is `[10.39663235, 43.7230066]`, at the centre of the mapped tower outline. The axes are X east, Y up and Z south, in metres. The footprint follows OpenStreetMap relation `12982355` version 29 and outer way `35667218` version 57. The replacement targets the tower's own geometry. The cathedral and neighbouring buildings are separate. [OSM tower relation](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/12982355) · [OSM outer way](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/35667218)

## Validation

- The GLB is 60,788 bytes and contains 2,486 vertices and 4,992 triangles in a single closed component. Its signed volume is 8,985.69 m³. There are zero boundary edges, non-manifold edges, orientation conflicts, degenerate faces or coincident vertices. The mesh has six through-openings at the top. Independent radial cuts found 30 relief peaks per loggia and about 1.32–1.43 m of radial relief. The 45 kB simplification visibly faceted the chamber and distorted the base and loggia rhythm, so the default keeps the higher resolution.
- Local Relleu Obert checks used vector buildings at 1:750 and 0°, OSM3D at 1:1,500 and 27°, and a partial crop at 1:1,500 and 9°. They confirmed ground contact, replacement of the tower parts and preservation of neighbouring buildings. The final audited 3MF at 1:750 was 136,095 bytes with 10,452 triangles.
- The local catalogue build and viewer checks covered search by alias, size, rotation, zoom and access to the GLB.
- At 1:750 the model is 75.6 mm high with a 20.7 mm plinth and 5.7 mm of crown offset. At 1:1,500 these become 37.8 mm, 10.3 mm and 2.9 mm. The bell openings are approximately 2.9 mm and 1.4 mm wide respectively. The smallest relief ridges can merge at the smaller scale. Slicer behaviour, support removal and physical printing remain to be checked.
