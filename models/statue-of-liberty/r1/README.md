# Statue of Liberty · r1

Liberty Island, New York, United States. The model includes Fort Wood, its terraces, Richard Morris Hunt's pedestal, and the statue with its tablet, drapery, seven crown rays and current torch. The island, museum and other buildings are separate. [National Park Service monument overview](https://www.nps.gov/stli/learn/statue-of-liberty-facts.htm)

## Measurements and reconstruction

- **Fort Wood and pedestal.** The star-shaped plan and orientation come from OpenStreetMap, with its two tiers of steps simplified from mapped widths. The stepped pedestal, shaft, colonnades and parapets follow HAER NY-138 sheets 15, 19, 21 and 30. The NPS sheets were delineated by Dana Lockett, Ananta Patel and Ryan Pierce. The terraced stonework has simplified pillars and solid interiors for printing. [OpenStreetMap Fort Wood](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/32965412) · [HAER four elevations](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny1251.sheet.00020a/) · [HAER east pedestal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny1251.sheet.00024a/) · [HAER south pedestal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny1251.sheet.00026a/) · [HAER section](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny1251.sheet.00035a/)
- **Figure.** Jerry Fisher's CC BY 4.0 photogrammetry of a nineteenth-century bronze supplies the draped body, face, tablet, crown and raised hand. This is a scaled bronze, while the monument itself is copper. A fit with separate axis scales and a yaw adjustment registered the bronze to 1,165 downsampled surface returns from the May 2017 NYC airborne survey, between 49.1 and 84.9 m NAVD88. Median and 90th-percentile distances from the LiDAR returns to the nearest scan vertices in the fit were 0.17 and 0.40 m. These are residuals of the registration on available points. The fitted figure was shifted 1.24 m across the front to register its pedestal with the mapped base. The simplified figure preserves the pose and folds. The seven modeled rays return to the registered bronze tip positions and taper from reinforced roots. [Fisher's scan and licence](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/statue-of-liberty-bronze-model-5b9aa58d51624f01b9009b52379bf18a) · [2017 survey metadata](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/64728) · [Liberty Island survey tile](https://gisdata.ny.gov/elevation/LIDAR/NYC_TopoBathymetric2017/970190.las) · [NOAA archived tile](https://noaa-nos-coastal-lidar-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/laz/geoid18/9306/20170504_970190.copc.laz)
- **Torch and height.** The upper torch shaft and balcony were adjusted against the airborne returns. The flame shape follows the 2011 HAER elevation and the 2017 point profile. The latter reaches 92.46 m NAVD88. The modeled tip is 93.05 m NAVD88, with the HAER silhouette and the NPS 46.05 m statue height used to estimate the apex above the returns. The flat contact plane uses 4.36 m NAVD88, the median of ground-height points sampled around the fort. This gives a model height of 88.69 m above the local contact plane. The NPS reports 92.99 m from ground to torch and 46.05 m from the statue base to the torch. Its ground reference is unspecified on the dimensions page. [NPS dimensions and current torch](https://www.nps.gov/stli/learn/historyculture/statue-statistics.htm) · [HAER south elevation with torch](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny1251.sheet.00026a/) · [USGS view of the surveyed statue](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/lidar-point-cloud-statue-liberty-0)

## Registration, source and reuse

The WGS84 anchor is `[-74.0445317, 40.689253]` at the pedestal. The GLB uses metres with X east, Y up and Z south. Its front is oriented at 146.9° true. The catalogue footprint is OpenStreetMap way `32965412` version 109. Replacement includes Fort Wood and the verified pedestal and stair parts. The museum and other neighbouring features have separate identities. [OpenStreetMap footprint](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/32965412)

The monument is in the public domain. The US National Park Service HAER drawings used here are federal measured drawings with no known rights restrictions. The adapted mesh is CC BY 4.0 with credit to Jerry Fisher for the bronze scan and Open Place Models contributors for the adaptation and architectural reconstruction. The catalogue footprint and OSM-derived plan in `layout.json` are © OpenStreetMap contributors under ODbL 1.0. [Library of Congress, public domain sculpture](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2020/11/the-original-lady-liberty/) · [Library of Congress HAER rights notice](https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/114_habs.html) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) · [OpenStreetMap attribution and ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

After installing the pinned dependencies, run `node scripts/generate-statue-of-liberty.mjs` from the repository root. The generator reads `layout.json` for dimensions, the reconstruction parameters and the fort plan, and `figure.json` for the registered, simplified geometry of the bronze. The latter records the archived source and its SHA-256. The output `print.glb` regenerated to SHA-256 `236d7a15c0060889a3e7206322c4565e9c520ccc2770fcd9d50254a6304a0ecc`.

## Validation

- The GLB is 81,156 bytes with 3,322 vertices, 6,656 triangles and one closed, consistently oriented component. Its signed volume is 64,604.37 m³. An independent audit found zero boundary edges, non-manifold edges, orientation conflicts, coincident vertices or degenerate faces. The bottom face and mapped Fort Wood footprint overlap by 99.98% intersection over union. All seven modeled rays reach within 0.07 m of their registered tip positions. A smaller figure reduced the drapery definition. Simplification also shortened some rays in the selected figure, so the generator restores their length from the registered tips.
- Local Relleu Obert checks used vector buildings at 1:1,000 and 0°, OSM3D at 1:2,500 and 27°, and a partial crop at 1:1,000 and 9°. The complete tests replaced 14 vector and 22 OSM3D parts, preserving 4 and 15 neighbouring features respectively. Ground contact, height limits and the hidden-buildings mode passed. The audited 3MF at 1:1,000 is 169,574 bytes with 13,032 triangles.
- The local catalogue viewer verified the Monument filter, search by alias, zoom, rotation, the reported 81.2 kB size and access to the GLB.
- The fort is about 96.6 × 96.5 m in plan. At 1:1,000 the model measures about 97 × 97 × 89 mm and at 1:2,500 about 39 × 39 × 35 mm. The crown roots measure around 0.37–0.43 mm across at 1:2,500, with finer tapered tips. Slicers may omit those tips at the smaller scale. Slicing and physical printing remain to be checked.
