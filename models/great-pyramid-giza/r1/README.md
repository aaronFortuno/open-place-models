# Great Pyramid of Giza · r1

Giza, Egypt. A simplified exterior of Khufu's pyramid as it survives, with stepped core masonry, the broad truncated summit and a small remnant above the summit terrace. The scope is the pyramid body. The entrances, interiors, outer pavement, survey mast and neighbouring monuments are outside this revision. [Egyptian Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities](https://egymonuments.gov.eg/monuments/the-great-pyramid/)

## Measurements and reconstruction

- **Base and orientation.** The generator uses a square of 230.363 m, the mean casing side estimated by the 2015 Glen Dash Foundation and AERA survey. It is rotated 3′54″ west of true north, the mean deviation in their table 3. This regularises the surviving base into one flat envelope. Above the first course the model retreats by Petrie's estimated 33.7 in (0.86 m) from the casing plane to the core at the corners. [Dash, 2015 survey, tables 1 and 3](https://aeraweb.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/aeragram16_2.pdf) · [Petrie, 1883, §26](https://petrieproject.com/book/the-pyramids-and-temples-of-gizeh)
- **Core masonry.** Petrie's plate VIII gives the northeast and southwest course levels relative to the pavement. The generator averages the two measured levels at each chosen boundary. It represents the 201 complete courses of Petrie's numbering with 73 bands, keeping the first eight and the thicker 35th course distinct and grouping most of the others in threes. The face lines interpolate between the measured base and summit. The broad midline recess on the north face uses Petrie's 37 in (0.94 m) estimate as its lower amplitude and tapers to the summit. The recess profile, step setbacks and slight batter of the risers are geometric approximations. Individual stones, casing fragments and erosion scars are omitted. [Petrie, 1883, §§23–26](https://petrieproject.com/book/the-pyramids-and-temples-of-gizeh) · [Petrie, plate VIII](https://www.ronaldbirdsall.com/gizeh/petrie/photo/plate8.html) · [Birdsall's annotated transcription](https://www.ronaldbirdsall.com/gizeh/errata/levels.html)
- **Surviving summit.** Petrie's four offsets produce an asymmetric 10.97 × 11.16 m terrace. Its modelled height of 137.377 m is the mean of his northeast and southwest elevations. The 2018 AERA ascent confirms a roughly 10 × 10 m terrace and a few higher stones. One simplified central mass represents the remains above the terrace. Its 1.12 m rise and approximately 1 m³ volume follow Goyon's reported tallest block, with a nominal combined height of 138.497 m above the modelled pavement. The remnant shape and exact position are approximate. Goyon's cumulative height of 138.745 m came from a separate survey. [Petrie, 1883, §25](https://petrieproject.com/book/the-pyramids-and-temples-of-gizeh) · [Goyon, 1978, pp. 406–407 and 413](https://www.ifao.egnet.net/bifao/078/23) · [Dash, 2018 survey ascent, pp. 16–20](https://aeraweb.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/aeragram19_1.pdf)

## Registration, source and reuse

The WGS84 anchor is `[31.134204029, 29.979179825]`, inferred by applying the published GPMP plan offset of 2.083 m east and 1.446 m north to the GPS position of station E1 measured in 2018. GLB coordinates are metres with X east, Y up and Z south. The height origin is the flat base. The catalogue records a pavement elevation of 59.9 m above sea level, the value given by Sullivan's table 1 from AERA data. This is a rounded elevation for placement in the terrain. [Dash, 2018, pp. 19–20](https://aeraweb.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/aeragram19_1.pdf) · [Sullivan, 2017, table 1](https://creativecoding.soe.ucsc.edu/courses/cmpm290A_ia/texts/Sullivan_JAMT_2017_lowres.pdf)

The catalogue footprint follows the survey-based model envelope. Its overlap with OpenStreetMap way `4420397` version 92 is 96.39% by intersection over union. That way is the verified replacement target. Its mapped polygon is included as a comparison in `measurements.json` under ODbL 1.0, © OpenStreetMap contributors. The simplified reconstruction and its generated footprint are CC BY 4.0, attributed to Open Place Models contributors. The geometry uses published numerical measurements and an original simplification. [OpenStreetMap footprint](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/4420397) · [OpenStreetMap attribution and ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Run `node scripts/generate-great-pyramid-giza.mjs` from the repository root. The script reads `measurements.json` and regenerates `print.glb` without extra dependencies. An optional output path also writes the catalogue entry. The mesh regenerates to SHA-256 `440cc60a90b8ffd18360edbb8236eb88f47d77ab7fa6db61ba42ac069dc466f0`.

## Validation

- The GLB is 19,164 bytes with 745 vertices and 1,486 triangles. An independent audit found one watertight, consistently oriented solid with positive signed volume of 2,605,616.53 m³. It found no coincident vertices, degenerate or duplicate faces, or non-manifold edges. The catalogue footprint matches the flat base within 4 mm when converted back to local metres with the WGS84 ellipsoid.
- Local Relleu Obert checks used Mapzen zoom 14 elevation data. At 1:2,000 and 0° in a 225 mm vector-data crop, the mesh replaced one OpenFreeMap feature and preserved four neighbours, including the pyramids of Hetepheres I and Meritites I. At 1:5,000 and 27° in a 200 mm OSM3D crop, it replaced one feature and preserved 483 neighbours, including Khafre's pyramid and the queens' pyramids. A partial crop at 1:2,000 and 13° remained closed.
- The opt-in `terrainBaseElevation` field requires support in the Relleu Obert client, available from v0.23.34. On the tested fallback surface model, the field corrects the embedded pyramid elevation beneath the catalogue mesh. The three cases placed the pavement at 59.90 m and the summit at 198.40 m above sea level. The uncorrected 1:2,000 control placed the summit at 243.73 m. If a local terrain model is available, the client uses that source for placement.
- The local gallery confirmed alias search for Khufu, the Egypt filter, the displayed 19.2 kB file size, zoom, keyboard orbit and the GLB link.
- At 1:2,000 the piece is about 115 × 115 × 69 mm, with a summit terrace about 5.5 × 5.6 mm. At 1:5,000 it is about 46 × 46 × 28 mm, with a terrace about 2.2 × 2.2 mm. The median band is 1.885 m high in the model, or 0.94 and 0.38 mm at these scales. The smaller treads and the summit remnant may be softened by a 0.4 mm nozzle at 1:5,000. Slicing and a physical print remain to be checked.
