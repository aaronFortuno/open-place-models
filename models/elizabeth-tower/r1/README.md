# Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben) · r1

The model covers the clock tower at the north-east corner of the Palace of Westminster. It includes the square masonry shaft, four clock dials, belfry, stepped iron roof, Ayrton Light lantern and cross and orb. Big Ben is the Great Bell inside the tower and is also the name commonly used for the landmark. The neighbouring palace wings and screen stay in the general building layer. [UK Parliament on the towers](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/architecture/palacestructure/towers-of-parliament/) · [Restoration and roof](https://www.parliament.uk/bigbenconservation/)

## Measurements and reconstruction

- **Dimensions.** The silhouette rises 96.3 m from the nominal grade. UK Parliament gives a footprint about 12 m square and dials 7 m across, with their centres 55 m above ground. The shaft is modelled at 12.2 m across at the plinth. Parliamentary evidence gives a 220 mm north-west lean at clock level. The model represents it with a small linear shift above the base. [Conservation project](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/big-ben/elizabeth-tower-and-big-ben-conservation-works-2017-/all-your-questions-about-the-works-answered/) · [House of Commons factsheet](https://www.parliament.uk/globalassets/documents/commons-information-office/Big-Ben.pdf) · [Written evidence on the lean](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/78705/html/)
- **Profile.** The tapering iron roof is interrupted by the Ayrton Light lantern and finishes in a slender spire. The dial recesses, hands, shaft bands, pointed windows and corner pinnacles are simplified as printable relief. The clock hands are illustrative. The roof profile was compared with the parliamentary tour and photographs and with the mapped levels of the principal roof stages. [Parliament virtual tour and photographs](https://virtualtours-assets.parliament.uk/elizabeth-tower/index.html) · [West façade photograph by David Iliff](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Clock_Tower_-_Palace_of_Westminster,_London_-_May_2007.jpg) · [OSM lower roof](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/363744574)
- **Terrain.** The Environment Agency 1 m bare-earth raster gives 4.671 m at the anchor and 4.057–6.168 m around the mapped perimeter. The model uses a nominal grade of 4.65 m. The flat underside sits 2.36–4.47 m below the raster at the 20 base vertices, allowing the terrain to cover the plinth footing. The survey crop uses the British National Grid and elevations in Ordnance Datum Newlyn. [Environment Agency LiDAR terrain data](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/01b3ee39-da3f-47b6-83da-dc98e73a461f/lidar-composite-digital-terrain-model-dtm-1m)

## Registration and reuse

The WGS84 longitude–latitude anchor is `[-0.12457363, 51.50070065]`. The façade axes are rotated 6.31151° clockwise from east and north. The GLB uses metres, with X east, Y up and Z south. Its base Y=0 is **1.70 m** in Ordnance Datum Newlyn. The model measures 15.04 × 15.03 m across its cardinal extents and 99.25 m high including the buried base.

The catalogue uses OSM way `123557148` version 51 and its tower family as the replacement identity. The OSM way for the low plinth `1134629402` lies wholly within the tower outline. The neighbouring screen `367642706` and the Palace of Westminster relation `1567699` are separate. ## Validation

- The local gallery and print loader accepted the 30,564-byte GLB. It has one closed component, 1,240 vertices, 2,476 triangles and a positive signed volume of 10,686.12 m³. The audit found zero boundary edges, non-manifold edges, winding conflicts, duplicate vertices or degenerate triangles. Each vertex has a single incident face ring. The 320 × 200 WebP thumbnail was checked visually.
- In Relleu Obert, OpenStreetMap at 1:500 replaced the tower’s mapped parts and retained the adjoining screen and palace. OpenFreeMap at 1:1,250 with a 26° rotation and a partial OpenStreetMap crop at 1:1,000 with a −19° rotation passed the mesh audit. In the OpenFreeMap test, the surrounding group retained five of six mapped fragments. With the engine’s vertical scaling accounted for, the apex was 100.950 m Newlyn and the model terrain at the anchor was 4.712 m. Height limits and hidden buildings were also checked.
- The 7 m dial scales to 14 mm across at 1:500 and 5.6 mm at 1:1,250. At 1:2,500 it is 2.8 mm across and the 0.74 m finial stem is about 0.30 mm thick, so a 0.4 mm nozzle can omit the smallest relief and the tip. Physical printing and slicing are pending.
